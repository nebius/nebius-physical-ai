Data and native mapping

Public input: youliangtan/so101-table-cleanup@f9b5e3f004d4dceee8bc8efbaf55cd013e46b707 (Apache-2.0). The source has 80 episodes; episode metadata totals 46,963 frames while info.json reports 47,513. The fixed split used episodes 0–63 (42,325 frames) for train and 64–79 (4,638) for held-out evaluation.

Two recorded cameras map to video.front and video.wrist; state/action map to the pinned native so100_dualcam configuration. The native reader decoded both terminal observations as [1,480,640,3], then produced N1.5 eagle_content processor input.

Source MP4s are AV1. The native runtime could not decode them, so a separate H.264 derivative was made with ffmpeg -map 0:v:0 -an -c:v libx264 -preset veryfast -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p. All 160 source and derivative video paths were retained; a checked sample preserved 640×480, 30 fps, and 855 frames. Conversion is lossy and does not preserve source pixels.

Limits

This proves data ingestion, recorded-video decoding, native N1.5 optimizer updates, held-out offline loss calculation, checkpoint serialization, and reload. It does not prove reward discrimination, policy improvement, robot success rate, physical deployment, or safety.
